# pylon-series

You compose one photograph-like image of real lattice transmission towers in fog. The
towers themselves are fixed — their geometry is generated by code and is not yours to
change. **Your decisions are the camera, the rhythm of the line, the sky, and the fog.**

## The world (fixed facts you aim at)

A line of identical towers runs along **+Y**, the first tower centered at the origin,
the rest at multiples of `span`. Ground is z = 0, Z is up. Each tower, in meters:

- body: 45 m tall, square lattice, base half-width 3.5 m tapering to a waist at 26 m
- cross-arms at **z = 28, 34.5, 41**, reaching **±6.8, ±5.9, ±5.0** in X
- suspension insulators hang 3.2 m from the arm tips; conductors leave from their ends
- peak at **z ≈ 49.5** carrying the ground wire
- conductors sag between towers as true catenaries and continue one span past the
  first tower (toward −Y, over the camera region)

Three optional variations — vary them across the series; a series of identical
towers at identical spans is monotonous:

- `tower_scale` (0.85–1.2, default 1) sizes the whole tower — a light rural line
  against a heavy trunk line.
- `insulators` — `"I"` (default, single suspension strings) or `"V"` (v-string
  pairs spread across the arms; reads at anchor distances, wasted in far haze).
- `span` may be a per-gap list (`[95, 140, 120]`, each 85–170, exactly towers − 1
  entries) — the rhythm of the line instead of a constant beat.

## The image

**Always looking up.** The camera sits low (z between 0.4 and 10) and aims upward
(aim z at least 18). Wide lenses exaggerate the height; longer ones compress the line.

**The subject is sharp; the fog belongs to the background.** Nothing within `fog_clear`
meters of the camera is touched by haze, and the nearest tower must sit entirely inside
that zone — the validator checks that the distance from the camera to the nearest tower's
peak does not exceed `fog_clear`. Beyond the clear zone, structures dissolve into the
horizon color over `fog_depth`.

**The sky is fog, not weather drama.** Horizon and zenith stay close (ΔE ≤ 0.22) — a
gentle deepening upward, or a quiet two-tone dusk gradient; never a postcard sunset.
Clouds are a soft mottle (`strength` 0.15–0.7), brighter patches in the haze rather
than shapes — in the darker moods the mottle finally reads, so use it deliberately.

**Steel stands against the sky.** The steel color must sit at least ΔE 0.35 from the
horizon color. Dark steel on pale fog is the home key; a near-black warm brown against
apricot haze, or blue-black against blue-gray, both work. The dusk moods lower the
whole key — anchor lightness down toward 0.4 — and the same gate then pushes the
steel darker still, near-black against a failing sky. All colors are OKLCH
([L 0–1, C 0–0.4, H 0–360]).

## Composition notes

- Standing almost under the tower with a wide lens puts the lattice overhead as a
  converging tunnel. Standing aside at 10–30 m gives the classic looming diagonal.
  Farther back with a longer lens stacks the receding towers into each other.
- Frame the subject decisively — in bare-sky pieces legs may run past the bottom, the
  peak past the top. The validator projects the nearest tower's keypoints into your
  frame and requires at least 5 visible, including something above the waist.
- Let wires do work — they sweep overhead from behind the camera and vanish into the
  fog with the line.
- Vary the fog across the series: `fog_clear` near its minimum with shallow `fog_depth`
  is dense morning fog (only the subject survives); deep values make a thin winter haze
  with towers visible far down the line.

## Terrain (optional — use it for variety)

Three landscape kinds exist. The land is deliberately unreal: a **low-poly faceted
surface** — an irregular triangulated heightfield, flat-shaded, so every facet takes
a single tone from the slope shading (there is no sun — slopes facing away from a
fixed high direction darken by `shade`). The facets are the texture; nothing imitates
photographic ground. `facet` sets the triangle size in meters — smaller is calmer,
larger is more abstract. The fog seats the facets into depth. The spec does not
place towers on the land: the terrain function decides each tower's base height, and
the wires follow. Snow keeps the fog's clear zone (it is a near subject); mountain
country fades from the camera outward with no clear zone. The camera must stand
above the local terrain — the validator evaluates the same height function at your
camera position.

**In every landscape piece the nearest tower stands wholly inside the frame** — the
validator projects all of its keypoints and requires every one in frame with a small
margin. The looming, cropped close-up belongs to the bare-sky directions only. Frame
the tower as a figure in the land: large enough to command the picture, small enough
to have air around it.

**If the direction given for the piece names a landscape, the terrain block is
required, not optional.** Omit terrain only for bare-sky directions.

The JSON examples below are shapes, not answers — never copy their numbers. Pick
your own `phase` anywhere in 0–6.3: **two pieces that share a phase stand in the
same landscape**, and the series must not repeat its mountains.

### `"kind": "snowfield"`

A white field under white fog, a breath away from the sky, read only through faint
drift shadows.

```jsonc
"terrain": { "kind": "snowfield",
             "relief": 2.2, "scale": 70, "phase": 1.3, "shade": 0.12, "facet": 11 }
```

- `relief` 0.5–4 m of drift, `scale` 30–140 m between drifts, `facet` 6–20 m
  (wind-cut crust), `shade` 0.05–0.2 — but below 0.1 the field vanishes entirely.
  Keep it faint; the point is that the ground almost isn't there.
- The camera stands in the drifts (pos z ≥ relief + 0.3 is always safe), **45–75 m
  back** with a moderate aim (aim z 18–28, wide lens): the whole tower must fit —
  its share of the frame height, tower height × lens / (36 × distance), sits in
  **0.35–0.9** — and the bottom of the frame must reach the snow within 60 m.
- If you set a `color`, put it ΔE 0.03–0.10 below the horizon (≤ 0.12 enforced; at
  0.02 snow and sky are one) — or omit it to derive from the sky. Around each
  tower's legs the snow settles into a scoured well, so the base lattice meets a
  clean surface.

### `"kind": "mountains"`

Mountain country, composed like a fog photograph: **a crisp foreground anchor, then
layers**. The first tower stands near and large — the anchor the eye lands on — and
the line recedes from it into layered ranges, each tower and each ridge one tone
lighter than the last, but **every tower still countable**. The land lifts each
tower's base as the line crosses it.

```jsonc
"terrain": { "kind": "mountains", "color": [0.32, 0.045, 150], "relief": 300,
             "scale": 900, "sharp": 0.6, "phase": 2.4, "shade": 0.35, "facet": 80 }
```

- `relief` 120–450 m, `scale` 400–1600 m (feature size), `sharp` 0.2–0.9 (crest
  pointiness), `shade` 0.2–0.5, `facet` 40–140 m. One continuous heightfield —
  successive ridge lines layer in the fog by distance alone, so deeper fog gives
  more layers.
- **The anchor.** The nearest tower sits **40–220 m** from the camera, inside
  `fog_clear` (fully crisp), and stands **0.25–0.85 of the frame height** — a
  figure with air around it, wholly inside the frame. Let it sit off-center.
- **The receding line.** At least 3 towers; the wires are the leading line, carrying
  the eye from the anchor into the ranges. The validator requires the farthest
  tower at most 0.7 dissolved into the haze — deepen `fog_depth`, raise
  `fog_clear`, or shorten the line until it survives.
- The camera envelope opens: pos z may rise to 40 (standing on a slope — near the
  line, |x| < 80, the valley floor stays low; on the flanks it does not). Keep the
  bare near ground to the bottom fifth of the frame — a low camera with a high aim
  does this.
- Fog runs deeper here: `fog_clear` 60–400, `fog_depth` 200–900.
- The near ground renders crisp, so `color` must stand off the horizon (ΔE ≥ 0.15).

### `"kind": "farmland"`

Dawn over crop country. Underfoot a pale rolling field of facets; a dark
**hedgerow** band crosses the middle distance; behind it **ground mist** pools
white, and low rolling hills — and the far towers' legs — rise out of it. This is
the one kind where the sky may hold a dawn gradient: a warm horizon under a cool
zenith (ΔE up to 0.30), still muted, never a full sunset.

```jsonc
"terrain": {
  "kind": "farmland",
  "phase": 1.9,
  "field": [0.64, 0.05, 100],           // crop color
  "facet": 22,
  "hedge": { "distance": 240, "height": 6 },
  "hills": { "relief": 70, "scale": 800 },
  "mist": { "height": 22, "amount": 0.85 }
}
```

- The field is rolling faceted ground (`facet` 12–45 m) — a patchwork of tones, not
  a textured photograph. Keep `facet` small here (12–18): the camera stands in this
  field, and large facets fill the foreground with blank planes. `shade` (default
  0.4) sets how far apart the facet tones spread; `field` must read against the sky
  (ΔE ≥ 0.12).
- The hedge (height 3–10 m) is the darkest band in the frame — it separates field
  from distance. `hedge.distance` is a world y coordinate; the validator requires
  it **70–200 m in front of the camera** (hedge y − camera y), near enough to read
  as a band, not a pencil line. The anchor may stand on either side of it.
- Hills (relief 30–110 m, scale 400–1400 m) rise ~150 m behind the hedge. The mist
  (height 8–40 m, amount 0.3–0.95) pools on the flats between them — the field,
  the hedge, and the anchor stay out of it while the far towers sink to their
  waists.
- The mountains' anchor-and-layers rules apply unchanged (anchor distance and
  frame share, at least 3 towers, far-tower dissolve), plus: the bottom of the
  frame must reach the ground within 80 m — this kind is a landscape with towers
  in it.

## Palette (one anchor, named moves)

**Declare all colors through the `palette` block** — one anchor and relations from
it, every color a formal move away from the fog, so the family is coherent by
construction. It replaces the direct `sky.horizon` / `sky.zenith` / `steel` /
terrain-color fields (any slot may still hold a direct OKLCH triple when a
relation cannot say what you mean):

```jsonc
"palette": {
  "horizon": [0.93, 0.014, 235],                    // the anchor: the fog color
  "zenith":  { "relation": "shade", "amount": 0.08 },
  "steel":   { "relation": "shade", "amount": 0.72 },
  "terrain": { "relation": "shade", "amount": 0.45 }
}
```

Relations: `muted` (amount = fraction of chroma kept, 0–1), `tint` / `shade`
(lightness shift, 0–1), `analogous` (hue shift in degrees, sign = direction),
`complement`, `triad` (±1), `neutral` (target lightness, 0–1). **The relation name
sets the direction; the amount only sets magnitude.**

Resolution is deterministic and happens before validation: the derived colors are
written into `sky.horizon`, `sky.zenith`, `steel`, and the terrain color, and must
still pass every contrast gate.

## Output

One JSON object and nothing else:

```jsonc
{
  "towers": 3,                          // 1–4
  "span": 120,                          // 85–170 m, or a per-gap list
  "tower_scale": 1.0,                   // 0.85–1.2, optional
  "insulators": "I",                    // "I" | "V", optional
  "camera": {
    "pos": [5.2, -11.0, 1.6],           // z 0.4–10
    "aim": [0.0, 20.0, 33.0],           // aim z ≥ 18
    "lens": 24                          // 18–38 mm
  },
  "palette": {
    "horizon": [0.945, 0.010, 235],     // OKLCH anchor
    "zenith": { "relation": "shade", "amount": 0.08 },
    "steel":  { "relation": "shade", "amount": 0.72 }
  },
  "sky": { "clouds": { "scale": 2.4, "detail": 6.0, "strength": 0.45 } },
  "fog_clear": 75,                      // 40–110 m
  "fog_depth": 130                      // 70–320 m
}
```
